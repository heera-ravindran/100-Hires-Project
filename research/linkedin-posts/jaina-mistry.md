I've been neglecting my personal inbox. It's been a busy few weeks. I'm not quite an inbox zero-er, but I try to be! 

The consistency of each of these newsletters is probably why they have pride of place in my primary tab vs. any other. 

Each sender consistently shows up like clockwork. With valuable, interesting content every single time. 

Consistency is probably one of the biggest and hardest goals to strive for in email. For a newsletter program it means you need to have:

- A calendar of mapped out content
- Depending on your cadence (anything less then 1 send every 2 weeks for a newsletter is pointless) actually written and ready to go content for the next 2 months, at least
- A close eye on performance and analytics, to see trends over time and adjust any planned content
- A steady promotional cadence to keep adding new subscribers 

I'm beginning to think about what a newsletter at Knak will look like. Specifically thinking about it through the lens of consistency. My plan: map out the narrative and create a process that helps me meet the needs for sending consistency with delivery of interesting, valuable content.

Post Text: The fold is a myth we borrowed from print and never let go.

Not saying the stuff above the fold in an email doesn't matter. It does.

It sets the tone and helps earn the scroll. But somewhere along the way, "above the fold" became the only thing that mattered and the rest of the email was left forgotten. 

(The opposite of a mullet email??)

What I keep reminding folks is that we, human beings, scroll now. It's a learned behaviour. Thanks to literally every app on your phone. Your subscribers aren't reading a newspaper. They're on their phones, thumb moving before they've even decided if they care.

I'm not saying you should bury your most important content at the bottom or make the top of your email the least compelling thing in it. Just make the whole email worth scrolling through. And include scroll-stopping moments.

What that could look like:

Your subject line, preview text, and headline should work together as a narrative. Not three versions of the same sentence. A thread that pulls someone in. As well we be accurately summarised by the AI inbox. Talking clarity over being too clever here.

Your primary CTA belongs up top, where intent is highest. But a saving CTA at the bottom? Tht one catches the people who needed a little more convincing.
That scroll-stopping moment could be a sharp visual, a pointed statement, or even a CTA. But it needs to be dramatic enough to break the visual journey of the scroll. Especially useful in those emails that take a couple of thumbs to scroll.

Ultimately, when you encourage a scroll you might get that click your "above the fold" content didn't quite manage.

It's the copy that stopped me in my tracks when I saw this email in my inbox. 

I've been deep in messaging and positioning land for the past few months. When I opened this email, it read like a textbook example of how to describe the values and outcome of a product. The translation of value prop to copy. 

Using language like "softest lounging layer" is tactile. It's how the cotton shorts feel. 

"Zero chafing. Zero ride up. Zero adjusting" are the benefits that speak to the buyer's pain points. 

"Coverage that disappeared under everything" is the outcome. 

This is how you relate your product to your audience. By using language that conjures memories and feelings.
